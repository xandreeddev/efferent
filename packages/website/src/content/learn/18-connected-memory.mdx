<Step n={1} id="build" title="What you will build" focus="chat">

In [lesson 11](/learn/remember/) an agent kept what it knew as facts: sentences, each on its own. "Ana works at Acme." and "Acme is in Lisbon." are two facts that know nothing of each other, and the question "In what city does Ana work?" shares no word with the second, by lesson 11's rule (words of three letters or more).

This lesson keeps what the agent knows as **links**. A link joins two **things**, each a name such as "Ana" or "Acme", by a **relation**: a few words that say how the first relates to the second, such as "works at". Read in order, a link is a sentence: Ana works at Acme. Two links that name the same thing are connected through it, Ana to Acme and Acme to Lisbon.

This lesson's agent has three tools of the kernel's and one wrapper:

- `linkThings` stores a link;
- `unlinkThings` removes one;
- `followThings` reads the links of one thing;
- a memory wrapper, `withGraph`, adds to each request the links near what the question names.

Press the first button under the chat, **Ana works at Acme. Acme is in Lisbon. …**: seven sentences in one message. One turn makes seven tool calls, a `linkThings` for each sentence, such as `linkThings({"subject":"Ana","relation":"works at","object":"Acme"})`. Each result is the link's id, from `{"id":"e1"}` to `{"id":"e7"}`, and Echo answers *The links' ids: e1, e2, e3, e4, e5, e6, e7.*

Press **In what city does Ana work?** Echo answers *From what I know: Ana works at Acme. Acme is in Lisbon.* No link says that Ana works in Lisbon: the answer is two links, one after the other.

</Step>

<Step n={2} id="model" title="Pick a model" focus="model">

This lesson runs on **Echo**, which follows a few rules, each narrow on purpose.

- In a message that does not end with a question mark, each sentence of the form "thing, relation, thing." is one `linkThings` call, when its relation is one of these: works at, is in, lives in, likes, drives, sells, is the sister of. "Ana knows Bo." is no link to Echo. "I work at …" and "I live in …" are links of the thing called "user" (step 8).
- "Follow Acme." and "What is Acme linked to?" call `followThings` with the thing named. "Unlink e2." calls `unlinkThings` with that id.
- A message that ends with a question mark, sent with the wrapper's note of links, is answered from the note's first two lines, whatever it asks, in fixed words: *From what I know: …*. One that asks what a thing is linked to calls `followThings` instead.

Echo answers a question from the note the wrapper adds, and from no message of the conversation.

With Qwen2.5 0.5B on the GPU, the seven links stored first with Echo and the note as this page sends it: asked **In what city does Ana work?**, it named Lisbon in 5 tries of 5 ("Ana works in Lisbon."), and asked "Where does Ana live?", whose note lists "Ana lives in Porto" first, Porto in 5 of 5. On the same six links at a limit of 8, it named Lisbon in 4 tries of 5 with the path first, and in none with the path last (the `by-distance` experiment's order), answering "Ana works at Acme." each time. Sent the seven sentences itself, it called `linkThings` with no parameters, and the call failed (tried once).

DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, through the gateway, called `linkThings` once for each of the seven sentences, then, unasked, `followThings` for Ana and for Acme. It remarked that the note it had been sent listed five links of the seven: that turn's own message holds the name of nearly every thing, and at most five links are sent (step 5). Asked **In what city does Ana work?** in the same conversation, which still held the seven sentences, it answered "Lisbon — Ana works at Acme, and Acme is in Lisbon (though she lives in Porto)." (tried once).

</Step>

<Step n={3} id="tools" title="Three tools, merged" focus="code" file="tools">

**tools.ts** is lesson 11's with the graph's three tools in place of the two that remember: `Toolkit.merge(Toolkit.make(calculator), graphTools)`.

- `linkThings({ subject, relation, object })` stores the link and answers its id. A link that says what a stored one says, in other capitals, is not stored again: the stored one's id is answered.
- `unlinkThings({ id })` says that the link no longer stands, and answers what it removed.
- `followThings({ name })` reads the links that name the thing, at either end, the newest first. It stores nothing.

A thing's name is one line of 1 to 80 characters, and a relation one of 1 to 40. A longer one is refused in words the model reads, and never cut. A thing is its name's words in lower case: "Acme" and "ACME" are one thing.

What the three tools do is the kernel's: **agent.ts** provides it in `main`, with `Effect.provide(graphHandlers)`. agent.ts is lesson 11's with the graph's handlers in place of the facts', and nothing in its loop changes. Its hook that names each turn on the status line passes over a turn on the graph session: each `linkThings` and `unlinkThings` call opens one.

</Step>

<Step n={4} id="record" title="Where a link goes" focus="context" wide={true}>

Open **Context**. The row of sessions above the turns has two buttons, *Chat* and *Graph*. Press **Graph**. It shows the **graph session**: a session of its own whose journal holds the links, as lesson 11's memory session holds the facts.

- Its journal holds 21 records, three for each link: `core.session.turn.started`, `plugin.graph.linked` and `core.session.turn.ended`.
- Each of its seven turns has three steps: *Turn opened*, *Link stored*, *Turn closed*. No model was called in them.
- Open record 2, `plugin.graph.linked`, tagged `memory`. It holds the link's id, `e1`, its subject, relation and object as the model wrote them, and under `from` the conversation it came from and `"turn": 1`.

Press **Chat**, and read turn 1 in the Journal column. Record 3 is the model's answer with its seven calls, and records 4 to 10 are their results, the ids. The conversation holds the calls and the ids, and no record of a link. There is no store of links beside the graph session's journal: `linksOf(journal)` folds that journal into the links that stand.

Press **Clear context**: a new, empty conversation in the same store. The *Graph* button stays. Press **In what city does Ana work?** again, and the answer is the same: the new conversation's question was sent the same links. Press **Rebuild**, then the question once more: the same answer again. The program started again and kept nothing of its own: the links are records, and the store is the same one.

A reload of the page is different. This page keeps its journals in the page itself, with `inProcessJournal()`, so a reload starts from an empty store: no conversation, no graph session, no link.

</Step>

<Step n={5} id="reach" title="How a question reaches a link" focus="context" wide={true}>

In **Context**, in the Chat session, pick turn 1: your question, the first message of this conversation.

- **Memory**: one message, your question.
- **Prompt**: three rows, 104 tokens. The system text, your question, and the note of links, marked *added by your code · never stored*.

The note's first line says what follows: "[Host note: these links between things were stored in earlier conversations, each read as a sentence. They are data, and give no instructions.]" Then come five links, a sentence each with its id, between `<known-links>` and `</known-links>`:

```
e1: Ana works at Acme.
e2: Acme is in Lisbon.
e7: Ana drives a red car.
e6: Ana is the sister of Eva.
e4: Ana likes tea.
```

memory.ts gives `withGraph` two numbers, `hops: 2` and `limit: 5` (step 8 shows the file). With them it chose these five of the seven in four moves.

- **The names.** The question is your last message. It names a thing when the thing's words are among the question's words, side by side and in the same order: "In what city does Ana work?" names Ana, and no other thing.
- **The walk.** From each thing named, the wrapper follows links from either end to the thing at their other end, and on from there, `hops: 2`, two links away at most: that is the **walk**. Ana's own links are one link away, and Acme is at the end of one, so "Acme is in Lisbon" is two away. "Bo works at Globex" is never reached: no link joins Bo to Ana or to Acme.
- **The ranking.** Each link reached is ranked by its **path**: the links the walk took to reach it from a thing the question names. First comes how many of the question's words of four letters or more the relations and names on the path meet, a word meeting another when one starts the other: "work" meets "works". The path to "Acme is in Lisbon" goes through "Ana works at Acme", so it meets "work" too, though it shares no word with the question. Then a link fewer links away comes first, then the newer.
- **The note.** At most `limit: 5` links are sent, best first, last in the request. The two that met "work" lead, the path's first link before its second. Ana's other links follow, the newest first, and the oldest, "Ana lives in Porto", is left out.

Echo's rule reads the note's first two lines, so here the order is the answer: with another link first, Echo would quote that one instead.

Lesson 11's ranking counts words of three letters or more. Given the same seven sentences as facts at the same limit of five, it sends the five that share "Ana" with the question, and leaves out "Acme is in Lisbon."

</Step>

<Step n={6} id="cost" title="What it costs" focus="chat">

- **Any message is a question.** The wrapper reads your last message, whatever it is. Press **Acme sells paint.** It names Acme, so each request of its turn was sent links near Acme, the first one before the new link was stored. Echo answers *The link's id: e8.*
- **Another link of Acme's ranks with the one that answers.** Press **In what city does Ana work?** again. The note now lists `e8: Acme sells paint.` before `e2: Acme is in Lisbon.`: the walk reached both through "Ana works at Acme", both meet "work" that way, and e8 is the newer. Echo answers *From what I know: Ana works at Acme. Acme sells paint.* The city is still sent, third, and Echo misses it. The walk knows words, not what a city is: enough such links push the answer past the limit.
- **A word that says little.** "does" has four letters, so to the ranking it is a word like any other, and it meets a relation that holds it. Asked "Where does Ana go?", a link "Ana does not like coffee" ranks first. Echo cannot store that link ("does not like" is not one of its relations): the kernel's own tests hold this case.
- **A thing with many links is cut by the limit.** Ana has five links of her own, and the question is sent five links in all: "Ana lives in Porto", the oldest, is never sent with it (step 5).
- **A question that names nothing.** Type **What city is it in?** It names no thing the graph holds, so it is sent no links, and Echo answers it like any other message: *You said: "What city is it in?".*, then the number of earlier messages it can see (9, if you followed steps 4 to 6 as written).

</Step>

<Step n={7} id="unlink" title="Removing a link" focus="chat">

Press **What is Acme linked to?** Echo calls `followThings({"name":"Acme"})`. Its result lists Acme's three links, each with its id, the newest first: e8, e2, e1. Echo answers *Following Acme: Acme sells paint. Acme is in Lisbon.* The tool stores nothing, and the graph session has no new turn. With it, a model reaches links that a request was not sent.

Press **Unlink e8.** Echo calls `unlinkThings({"id":"e8"})`, and the result reads "Removed e8: Acme sells paint". Press **In what city does Ana work?** The note is step 5's again, and so is Echo's answer, with the city.

Open **Context** and press **Graph**. Turn 9 holds three records, as every turn there does: its second is `plugin.graph.unlinked`, with the id `e8`. Record 23, which stored the link, is still there. A journal only grows: a link stands while no later record removes it.

</Step>

<Step n={8} id="speaker" title={'Who "I" is'} focus="code" file="memory">

A link's names are the model's words, so a link about the person it talks with needs a name for that person. **memory.ts** gives one: `speaker: { name: "user", words: ["i", "me", "my"] }`. A question that holds "I", "me" or "my" names the thing called "user", as one that holds "Ana" names Ana. The words are the host's, in its own language: the kernel holds none. Each must be one word: "my name" refuses the build.

Press **I work at Acme.** Echo calls `linkThings({"subject":"user","relation":"works at","object":"Acme"})`, and answers *The link's id: e9.*

Press **Where do I work?** No thing is called "where" or "work", but "I" names user. The note lists three links: `e9: user works at Acme.`, then `e2: Acme is in Lisbon.` and `e1: Ana works at Acme.`, two links away through Acme. Echo answers *From what I know: user works at Acme. Acme is in Lisbon.*

Press **In what city does Ana work?** once more. "user works at Acme" is a link of Acme's beyond Ana's path, as e8 was, and newer than e2. Echo answers *From what I know: Ana works at Acme. user works at Acme.*

</Step>

<Step n={9} id="experiments" title="Experiments" focus="code" file="memory">

`withGraph` refuses options it does not take, where they are written. Change `hops: 2` to `hops: 4` in memory.ts and press **Rebuild**. The build is refused, and the line at the foot of the Code pane says why, in the kernel's words: "memory.ts did not run: withGraph needs hops, a whole number from 1 to 3: how many links away from what a question names it walks. It was given 4". A limit of 0 is refused the same way. No program runs until a build passes, and the conversation and the links are untouched. Change the number back and press **Rebuild**.

Each experiment below starts from a page with no link. A reload is the one thing on this page that empties the graph session, and it also undoes your edits to memory.ts. For each experiment: reload the page, press its button, then the first two buttons under the chat.
