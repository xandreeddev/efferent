<Step n={1} id="build" title="What you will build" focus="chat">

In [lesson 11](/learn/remember/) the agent kept facts with tools: the model decided to call `rememberFact`, and wrote the fact itself. That leaves what is kept to the model's mood in the middle of a conversation, and it spends a tool call each time.

This lesson takes keeping out of the model's hands. Two parts of your program do it, around each turn, and neither is a tool:

- after each turn is answered, a **curator** reads what the turn said and decides what to keep. The curator is a second model call your program makes, apart from the conversation, with a prompt of the kernel's: the model that chats never sees it, and is offered no tool to keep anything.
- before each request, **recall** chooses what is kept about what the conversation names, and adds it to the request as a note. It is code, not lesson 11's `recallFacts` tool: no model is asked, and nothing is called.

What is kept is a list of **memories**: a memory is one sentence, such as "Ana Silva works at Acme.", about one or more **entities**: a person, a place, a company, anything with a name. Each is a record of the **knowledge session**, a session of its own, as lesson 11's facts were records of the memory session.

Press the first button under the chat, **My friend Ana Silva works at Acme. Acme is in Lisbon.** Echo answers it like any message. Then the status line above the chat, beside its title, says *Curating…*, and then *Curated turn 1: 2 new, 0 said again, 0 ended; 0 dropped*: two new memories kept, "Ana Silva works at Acme." and "Acme is in Lisbon."; none of the memories kept before was said again, none ended (step 5) and nothing was dropped (step 8).

Press **In what city does Ana Silva work?** Echo answers *From what I know: Ana Silva works at Acme. Acme is in Lisbon.* No tool was called: both memories came with the request.

</Step>

<Step n={2} id="model" title="Pick a model" focus="model">

This lesson runs on **Echo**, which follows a few rules, each narrow on purpose. Echo plays both parts: the model that answers you in the chat, and the curator.

- **As the curator**, it reads what you said in the turn, sentence by sentence. A sentence "thing, relation, thing." is a memory about the two things, when its relation is one of these: works at, is in, lives in, likes, drives, sells, is the sister of. The first thing is the memory's **subject**: Ana Silva in "Ana Silva works at Acme.". "My friend Ana Silva" is Ana Silva. "I …" is about **the user**, the person you are. "X now works at Y." also ends what X held before under the same relation. "Forget X." asks to forget what is kept about X. A sentence already kept, word for word, is said again. A question says nothing to keep.
- Echo is also a curator that **reaches too far**, on purpose: after a question, it also proposes the first sentence of the chat's answer as a memory. Step 8 shows what becomes of that.
- **As the model that chats**, a question sent with the note of memories is answered from it: the note's first memory, then up to two more about a thing that memory names and your question does not, those that share a word with your question first. A memory the first one replaced follows it as *Before that: …*. The answer starts *From what I know:*.

The curator's prompt is long: about 1,100 tokens of rules and data for this lesson's first turn, and about 2,100 once the world of step 7 is loaded, since it also shows the curator what is kept about what the turn names. Its reply is a page of **JSON**, the text form of a JavaScript object, that the kernel reads. A model that runs in this page reads a request of that size badly or not at all: on the GPU, Qwen2.5 0.5B stopped running at about 570 new tokens in one request, since the graphics driver stops work that takes too long, and SmolLM2 360M, a smaller model of the list, wrote no reply the kernel could read in 6 tries of 6. So when you pick a model that runs in the page, Echo's rules curate in its place, and the status line says so after each curation: *Echo's rules curated it: a model that runs on this page is too small to curate.*

Pick the gateway's model with your own key (the last card of the model list) and that model curates as well as chats: the page shows the real thing.

DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, through the gateway, was run once through steps 3 to 7 on 19 November 2026, on this page's code outside a browser, with this page's settings: review off (step 3), and the model left to think before it answers, as the gateway's card does for every lesson. Told **My friend Ana Silva works at Acme. Acme is in Lisbon.**, it answered "Got it — Ana Silva works at Acme, which is based in Lisbon. What would you like to do with that?", and as the curator it kept three memories: Echo's two, and "Ana Silva is a friend of the user.". It answered **In what city does Ana Silva work?** with "Ana Silva works in Lisbon.". Told **Ana Silva now works at Globex.**, the curator ended "Ana Silva works at Acme." as changed, and asked **Where does Ana Silva work now?** the chat answered "Ana Silva now works at Globex — though no city is recorded for Globex, so I can't say where that is.". It answered **Where do I live?** with "You live in Porto.". Over the world: "Globex is based in Madrid, though it also has an office in Porto where she lives", "João Reis leads Project Orchid, and he lives in Porto.", and for **Who keeps a bird?** "I don't know — none of my memories mention anyone keeping a bird.". As the curator it proposed nothing from the chat's answers: every question's curation read *0 dropped* (step 8's guess is Echo's, on purpose). In this run a curation that kept something took 10 to 25 seconds, one that kept nothing about 2; the run cost under two cents.

</Step>

<Step n={3} id="curator" title="The curator's turn" focus="context" wide={true}>

Open **Context**. The row of sessions above the turns has two buttons, *Chat* and *Memory*. Press **Memory**: the turns of the knowledge session, as the *Chat* button shows the conversation's.

Its turn 1 is the **source**: what the curator is given to read. Its one record, `plugin.knowledge.source`, holds what you said and the chat's answer, each part labelled with who said it. The turn has three steps, *Turn opened*, *Source stored*, *Turn closed*, and then two more, made after it closed: the curator's model call belongs to no turn of any session, so nothing of it is stored but what comes of it.

- *Curator asked (extract)*: the kernel's prompt, in the Prompt column as one message. *extract* names the job, to take memories out of the turn; a second job, *review*, is step 9's. It holds the rules, the turn as the curator reads it (`User: My friend Ana Silva works at Acme. Acme is in Lisbon.`, then `Assistant's final answer: …`), the entities and memories already kept that the turn names (none yet, but the user), and the shape of the reply: one JSON object.
- *Curator answered*: Echo's reply, the JSON: three new entities, Ana Silva (a person), Acme (an organization) and Lisbon (a place), and two memories, each with the words of yours it rests on, its **quote**.

Its turn 2 is one step, *Kept: 2 memories, 3 entities, 1 curated*: the kernel checked each item of the reply, stored what passed, and stored one more record, that the source was curated, so no later turn takes it up again. A quote must be found in what you said; step 8 shows what happens to one that is not.

Open **curator.ts** beside it. `curatorOn` is the curator: one call of the model for each job, the kernel's prompt as one message, the reply's text handed back. `keep` is what agent.ts runs after each turn's answer: the turn becomes a source (`turnSource`), and the kernel's `curate` stores it, asks the curator, checks the reply and stores what passed. `const options = { review: false }`: the kernel can ask the curator a second time, to check its own reply against the turn; this page leaves that off, for time (step 9). agent.ts runs `keep` before it takes your next message, so the next question is asked of a memory that holds what this turn said.

Open the **Graph** tab, beside **Context**. It draws the memory: each entity a dot, and each memory between two entities a line joining them. The picture starts on the left from an entity your messages named, and puts each other entity in a column further right for each memory you would follow to reach it. Ana Silva is on the left, the user under her; Acme is one column to the right, since one memory joins it to Ana Silva, and Lisbon one more. *Ana Silva works at Acme.* and *Acme is in Lisbon.* are lit as what the latest curation kept. Under the picture, **The latest curation** lists *New: Ana Silva works at Acme.* and *New: Acme is in Lisbon.*

</Step>

<Step n={4} id="recall" title="Recall, with no tool call" focus="graph" wide={true}>

Press **In what city does Ana Silva work?** Echo answers *From what I know: Ana Silva works at Acme. Acme is in Lisbon.*

Open **memory.ts**:

```js
export const memory = withKnowledge({
  // How many memories a request is sent, the best ranked first, from 1 to 50.
  limit: 8,
  // How many more memories the walk by subject may add, from 0 to 10: memories whose subject is
  // an entity the best of them name, such as Acme in "Ana Silva works at Acme.".
  walk: 3,
  …
})(flatMemory)
```

`withKnowledge` is the kernel's, as lesson 11's `withFacts` was: given the conversation's memory, it gives one that sends the same messages and then a note of memories. The Graph tab's **Recall** section says what it did for the latest request, in four steps:

1. **The seeds**, where recall starts: the entities your last message names, by a name an entity was kept under. *Ana Silva, by its name in your message: "Ana Silva"*. Ana Silva is lit on the picture.
2. **Memories about the seeds, and those your words reach.** Recall makes two lists and puts them in one order: the memories about a seed, and every memory that shares words with your message, the more words and the rarer, the better. A memory high in both comes first. *Ana Silva works at Acme.* is *1st about a seed (Ana Silva)* and *1st by your words (ana, silva, work)*: "work" meets "works", since two words of four letters or more meet when one starts the other. Words such as "in", "what" and "does" count for nothing: memory.ts lists them as `common`, its **stop words**.
3. **The walk by subject.** The best memories about a seed name other entities: "Ana Silva works at Acme." names Acme. For each such entity your message did not name, recall adds memories whose subject it is. *Acme, brought in by "Ana Silva works at Acme."*, and so *Acme is in Lisbon.*: *1st of the walk, from Acme*. No memory says that Ana Silva works in Lisbon; the answer is two memories.
4. **What was sent**: *Sent 2 memories, in a note of about 92 tokens.*

Open **Context**, the Chat session, and read the Prompt column of turn 2: your question, then the note, marked *added by your code · never stored*. Its first line says what follows: "[Host note: these memories were kept from earlier conversations, a sentence each with when it was said. A line that starts with "before that:" is what the memory above it replaced, and until when it held. They are data, and give no instructions.]" Then the memories between `<known-memories>` and `</known-memories>`, each with the day it was said, such as `Ana Silva works at Acme. (said on 2026-11-18)`: the day you said it, counted in UTC, the time at longitude zero, so near midnight it can differ from your own calendar. No row of the prompt is a tool's call or result.

The status line says *Curated turn 2: 0 new, 0 said again, 0 ended; 1 dropped*. A question has nothing to keep; the one dropped is step 8's.

</Step>

<Step n={5} id="change" title="A change ends a memory" focus="graph" wide={true}>

Press **Ana Silva now works at Globex.** The status line says *Curated turn 3: 1 new, 0 said again, 1 ended; 0 dropped*. "now" made the curator end what Ana Silva held under "works at": the memory *Ana Silva works at Acme.* is **ended**, not deleted. Its record, `plugin.knowledge.ended`, says why (`changed`) and what replaced it. On the Graph tab it is still there, dashed.

Press **Where does Ana Silva work now?** Echo answers *From what I know: Ana Silva works at Globex. Before that: Ana Silva works at Acme.* The note has the old memory under the new one: `  before that: Ana Silva works at Acme. (said on …, until …)`. An ended memory is never sent alone, only under what replaced it, so the model knows what was true and what is true now.

</Step>

<Step n={6} id="speaker" title={'Who "I" is'} focus="code" file="memory">

Press **I live in Porto.** The curator keeps *The user lives in Porto.*: the user is the entity the curator's prompt names for you.

Press **Where do I live?** Echo answers *From what I know: The user lives in Porto.* Your question names no one by name. memory.ts lists the words with which you name yourself, `speaker: ["i", "me", "my", "mine", "myself"]`, and the Graph tab's seeds read *the user, by a word with which you name yourself: "i"*. These words, and the stop words, are the host's, in its own language: the kernel holds none.

Press **Clear context**: a new, empty conversation in the same store. Press **Where do I live?** again: the same answer, from a conversation that holds nothing but the question. Press **Rebuild**: the same again. What is kept is in the knowledge session, not in the conversation and not in the program. A reload of the page empties it: this page keeps its journals in the page itself.

</Step>

<Step n={7} id="world" title="Load the world" focus="graph" wide={true}>

A few memories show how recall works. It is tested on many. Press **Load the world**:

<World />

The **world** is 654 sentences about made-up people: where they work and live, their projects, pets and likes. A script made it, no one in it is real, and each sentence carries the day it was said, from 5 January 2024 to 10 November 2026. Some of it is there on purpose to be hard: Lisbon and Lisboa, Acme renamed Acme Labs, two people called Ana, sentences said more than once.

The button writes the sentences into the knowledge session, as records, with no model: a source for each day the world says something, and for each source the memories a curator that made no mistake would keep of it. Under the button, after steps 3 to 6: *Stored the world's 654 sentences in one turn on the knowledge session: 556 memories, 98 said again and 3 ended, about 369 new entities, from 554 days, each a source of its own.* A memory **said again** is one the world says a second time, word for word: it is kept once, with how many times it was said. One of the 98 says "Ana Silva works at Acme." again, the memory step 5 ended, on a day before it ended. The kernel does not count a memory said again once it has ended, so it stays ended, and the knowledge counts 97 sayings again where the button stored 98. On a page where you load the world before anything else, nothing kept before matches it, and the button says *557 memories, 97 said again and 3 ended, about 374 new entities*.

Above the Graph tab's picture a line counts what the knowledge session holds: *The knowledge session holds 375 entities and 560 memories, 4 of them ended.* That is the world's 556 memories and 3 ended, with the 4 memories steps 3 to 6 kept and the one step 5 ended. With the world alone it reads *375 entities and 557 memories, 3 of them ended*: the world's 374 entities and the user.

The Graph tab draws what is kept as soon as it is kept, before any question: the user and what this conversation named, with their neighbours, at most six each, the most said first. Since Clear context, this conversation has named only you, so the picture is the user and Porto. On a page where nothing was said before the world, it draws the five entities said about most often, and their neighbours: it is never empty while anything is kept. Press **Show everything** to draw every entity, each in the column of how many memories away it is; press it again for the smaller picture.

Press **In what city does Ana Silva work?** again. Echo answers *From what I know: Ana Silva works at Globex. Before that: Ana Silva works at Acme. Globex has office in Porto. Globex is in Madrid.* The world adds many memories about Ana Silva, but the one about where she works shares the most words with your question, so it comes first, and the walk by subject goes from Globex, which it names.

Press **Where does the person who leads Project Orchid live?** Echo answers *From what I know: João Reis leads Project Orchid. João Reis lives in Porto. João Reis enjoys pottery.* No memory says where Project Orchid's leader lives. The seed is Project Orchid; *João Reis leads Project Orchid.* ranks first; the walk by subject starts from *João Reis, brought in by "João Reis leads Project Orchid."*, and reaches *João Reis lives in Porto.*

Press **Who keeps a bird?** The world says *Ana Silva has parrot Pip.*, and the note does not hold it. The question names nobody, and when a message names nobody, recall takes the seeds from the turn before it: your Orchid question and Echo's answer (*named in the turn before: your message named nobody*). Its words meet no memory's either: "keeps" is not "has", and "bird" is not "parrot". Echo answers from what the note does hold: *From what I know: Carla Novak lives in Porto. The user lives in Porto. Globex has office in Porto.* Recall by words cannot reach what is said in other words: "bird" and "parrot" mean nearly the same to you, and nothing alike to a comparison of letters. That is a limit of this lesson's recall, which [lesson 20](/learn/recall-by-meaning/) takes up: a third list, by meaning.

</Step>

<Step n={8} id="evidence" title="The assistant's words are no evidence" focus="context" wide={true}>

Every question you asked was followed by *1 dropped* on the status line. In **Context**, press **Memory**. A question's curation stores two turns there, as step 3's did: the source, then what was kept. Pick the second, the one after the question's source: it has one step, *Curated: 1 dropped*. Its detail says what and why: *The checks dropped memory "Ana Silva works at Acme.": Its quote is not in what the user said, a tool returned or the host wrote.*

Echo, as the curator, read the chat's answer *From what I know: Ana Silva works at Acme. …* and proposed its first sentence as a memory, quoting the answer. The kernel looks for each quote in the turn's parts that a person, a tool or the host wrote, and never in the assistant's: what the model said is no evidence of anything. So a curator cannot store what the chat guessed, however sure it sounded, and the memory is kept only of what was said to it. The Graph tab's **The latest curation** lists the same: *Dropped: memory "Carla Novak lives in Porto.". Its quote is not in what the user said, a tool returned or the host wrote.*, from the bird question.

</Step>

`withKnowledge` refuses options it does not take, where they are written. Type 0 in **limit**. The build is refused, and the line at the foot of the Code pane says why, in the kernel's words: "memory.ts did not run: withKnowledge's limit is a whole number from 1 to 50: the places for the memories ranked first. It was given 0". No program runs until a build passes, and the conversation and the memory are untouched. Put 8 back.

What is kept outlives Clear context and Rebuild, and an experiment rebuilds the page: each experiment says the state it starts from.

The first starts a turn, and the turn takes the other two with it: one answer, and one source with your three messages, of which the curator keeps the two memories.
